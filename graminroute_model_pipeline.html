GraminRoute AI model pipeline showing 6 stages from raw data input to final output

1
Raw Input — Shop Data
What we know about the retailer right now
7 base features
current_stock daily_sales lead_time_days profit_margin shelf_life is_festival → days_to_festival credit_score
What changes from current: is_festival (binary 0/1) is replaced with three richer features — days_to_festival (continuous, e.g. 8), festival_spike_factor (e.g. 2.8 for Diwali), and product_festival_affinity (does this product spike for this festival?). These feed from a hardcoded festival calendar mapping Indian festivals to affected products and historical demand multipliers.
↓ normalize to [0,1]
2
XGBoost — Individual Risk Score
Looks at this shop in isolation, ignores neighbors
replaces quantum VQC
What it does: Takes the 7 normalized features and predicts a raw risk score for this shop alone. Trained on the Indian retail dataset. Handles non-linear feature interactions (e.g. low stock + high sales + festival nearby = exponentially higher risk than any one factor alone).

Why XGBoost over quantum: Same 7 features go in, similar output comes out — but XGBoost is interpretable (feature importance plot shows "festival_spike_factor is the #1 driver"), faster, and production-proven at Flipkart-scale.

Output: raw_risk_score = 0.74 — but this ignores what's happening in neighboring villages.
↓ risk score becomes a node feature
3
GATv2 GNN — Spatial Awareness
Looks at what's happening in neighboring villages
kept, quantum removed
Graph structure: Every village/shop is a node. Road connections are edges. Edge features = road type (highway gets higher attention weight, dirt road gets lower).

What it does: Takes the XGBoost risk scores for all nodes + road graph. The attention mechanism asks "which neighbors matter most for predicting this shop's demand?" If Village A (highway-connected, 2km away) is already spiking, GATv2 propagates that signal to Ramesh's shop — warning him before his own sales spike.

Two GATv2 layers: Layer 1 aggregates immediate neighbors (4 attention heads). Layer 2 aggregates the aggregated neighborhood. Sigmoid output → final spatially-aware risk score.

Output: spatial_risk_score = 0.82 — higher than raw because neighboring villages are already running low.
↓ risk score + stock + demand
4
Festival Demand Predictor
Calculates days until stockout given upcoming festival
new component
Inputs: spatial_risk_score + current_stock + daily_sales + festival_spike_factor + days_to_festival.

What it calculates: predicted_daily_demand = daily_sales × spike_factor
days_until_stockout = current_stock ÷ predicted_daily_demand

Example: Ramesh has 12 units of cooking oil. Normal daily sales = 3 units. Diwali spike factor = 2.8. Predicted demand = 8.4/day. Days until stockout = 12 ÷ 8.4 = 1.4 days. Diwali is 8 days away. He needs to restock in the next 24 hours.

Recommended order qty: (days_to_festival × predicted_daily_demand) - current_stock + safety_buffer
↓ all signals combined
5
XGBoost Recommender — Distributor Ranking
Which distributor to use and why
replaces rule matrix
Inputs: spatial_risk_score + days_until_stockout + recommended_qty + days_to_festival + credit_score.

Trained on: Synthetic labels generated from the existing rule-based scoring matrix. XGBoost learns the same logic but generalises to edge cases the rules don't cover.

Output: Ranked list of distributors with confidence scores. High risk → FastTrack ranked #1 (speed), low risk → Budget Movers ranked #1 (cost).

Feature importance reveals: days_until_stockout and spatial_risk_score are the top two drivers. credit_score matters more than profit_margin for distributor willingness to extend credit terms.
↓ optimized delivery
6
Routing Engine — OR-Tools VRP
Optimal truck route across pooled orders
new component
Inputs: Pool of shops needing delivery (from DBSCAN + Clarke-Wright) + PostGIS road distance matrix + urgency scores (from risk model).

What it does: Solves the Vehicle Routing Problem — given N shops to deliver to, what's the optimal visit sequence for the truck? High-risk shops (days_until_stockout < 2) are given tight time windows so OR-Tools prioritises them.

Highway weighting: Distance matrix uses road-type-weighted costs. Highway edges cost less → OR-Tools routes through highways naturally, same as how Google Maps prefers them.

Output: Ordered stop sequence — Hub → Shop C → Ramesh → Shop A → Shop D → Hub.
